# Main Quest Roadmap — Part 1: The Missing Heroes

Design date: 30 September 2026. Scope: detailed narrative and feature design for Main Quests 1–3. This is a writing and implementation roadmap; it does not change the playable game yet.

## 1. Author requirements and campaign structure

The following requirements replace the short opening chapter's pacing:

- The campaign has four parts, with seven main quests in each part.
- Seven substantial main quests precede Kethrax. He is the boss of **Main Quest 8**, encountered when the player is around **level 40 or higher**.
- Kethrax is a strong, established enemy commander among other commanders. Defeating him does not end the game, the Holy War, or Aljay's captivity.
- The Holy War has three competing sides: **The Descendants**, **Humankind**, and **Forsaken Ones**. Their strongest champions and races must matter to its scale.
- Aljay, Roydo, and Paul David failed in their greater undertaking, but their actions gave humans lasting proof that they could resist.
- Investigating Aljay's disappearance is a central thread of the player's journey.
- Paul David tells the story of Aljay and Roydo in **Main Quest 3**.
- The player receives a rank promotion after Quest 3. Future promotion quests are chosen only by the author. Completing an ordinary main quest must not imply promotion.

**Numbering assumption:** Part 1 contains Quests 1–7 and prepares the confrontation; Quest 8 opens Part 2. Part 2 is Quests 8–14, Part 3 is Quests 15–21, and Part 4 is Quests 22–28. This preserves both the specified quest count and Kethrax's position. There is no extra, unnumbered Kethrax finale.

All new faction explanations, character motives, quest titles, encounters, timing targets, and mechanics below are **proposed**, not additional author-approved canon. Existing names and history are retained where compatible. Quests 4–7 and Parts 2–4 are deliberately not scripted here.

## 2. The war and why the three heroes matter

### Three sides with incompatible aims

| Side | Proposed belief and objective | Why it fights both other sides | What the player sees first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Descendants** | Bloodlines that claim descent from Jre's ancient rulers. Their ruling houses consider control of the Aether and the ancient creatures an inherited right. Their promised peace requires every other people to submit. | Independent human guilds challenge their authority. The Forsaken steal and corrupt the inheritance they claim. | An emissary demands that humans surrender an old crossing and its ward. Later, a Descendant force attacks Forsaken captors while also denying humans passage. |
| **Humankind** | Towns, guilds, and island governments defending their right to live without surrendering to either power. Human Aether users can rise beyond inherited limits. | The Descendants demand obedience; the Forsaken turn people and heroes into captives, servants, or sources of power. | Farmers protecting neighbors, rival guilds sharing supplies, and officials disagreeing over whether survival requires submission. |
| **Forsaken Ones** | Peoples, broken oath-keepers, and transformed beings who reject the ancient order and seek to remake Jre through stolen power and the return of the Age of Wrath. Their leaders call bondage a necessary path to liberation. | The Descendants embody the order they want destroyed. Free humanity supplies heroes and organized resistance they cannot safely leave independent. | Search parties taking witnesses alive, devices that extract Aether, and orders to recover a particular black lance fragment. |

These are political and religious war factions containing different peoples, rather than three interchangeable monster types. Their racial composition remains open for the author. Their beliefs do not dictate every individual's behavior: dissenters, captives, and defectors can exist without turning the campaign into a fourth side.

The war is holy because it concerns who may inherit, use, or remake the Aether and the ancient Binding. Sacred sites are also real infrastructure: a captured shrine can close a road, break a town's ward, or help wake an ancient creature. The player should see those consequences before receiving a long history lesson.

**Proposed reconciliation with existing lore:** the Kharvenn Dominion, Sulvane Theocracy, and Ysmer become outside powers whose ruling houses claim Descendant authority. The Forsaken Legion is a formation within the Forsaken Ones, and Kethrax commands its chain-binding forces in this region. The old Pact of Wrath can remain an agreement among those outside powers; it no longer makes the Forsaken their obedient army. Past exchanges of weapons, captives, or information can be temporary bargains between enemies. The Ashen Circle's exact allegiance remains unresolved in these three quests. This explicitly replaces the old two-sided account in which every enemy serves one united Pact.

### Strength without losing the player’s role

The leaders and great champions should feel capable of changing a battlefield. Use aftermath, distant clashes, surviving witnesses, and selective cinematic scenes to establish that scale. Do not fill the opening roads with level-200 enemies that a new hero inexplicably defeats.

The first three quests concern supply routes, missing people, a surviving relic, and truthful testimony. Their outcomes matter to the war even though the player is still much weaker than its greatest champions.

The Dawnbreakers remain Class SX legends above level 200. Their existing appearances remain: Aljay's black and crimson armor and enormous lance, Roydo's white-gold armor and hammer, and Paul David's storm-blue coat and sword. The player is not granted their strength by touching a relic.

**Why Kethrax could take Aljay:** the Three were already protecting an evacuation; trusted Accord troops disabled their ward from within; prepared binding anchors suppressed their Aether; and Kethrax used specialized chains while the heroes were separated. His role was prepared capture, not winning a fair duel against three SX heroes. Aljay's counterattack still left the wound in his chest.

**Why a level-40+ player can eventually fight him:** the later investigation must identify and disable his local support, explain how the shard exposes that old wound, and provide a viable approach. Preparation removes unfair battlefield advantages; it does not turn Kethrax into a weak enemy or give the player Aljay's power. The actual Quest 8 encounter remains a later design task.

### What the Three lost, and what survived

Their greater aim was to keep Salmonan's people independent while preventing the ancient powers from turning it into a battlefield. At the Weeping Causeway, they failed to secure the crossing and stop the enemy foothold. Aljay was taken, Roydo disappeared, and Paul David returned unable to wield his blade.

They nevertheless held long enough for civilians and surviving defenders to escape. Those people became Wyman's scouts, Malasugue's teachers and craftspeople, and Olivar's boat crews. Their continued resistance is the heroes' unfinished achievement.

The official accusation that Aljay willingly joined the Forsaken competes with living memories of what he did. Hope is visible in actions: a farmer keeps a shelter ready, a former refugee takes a night watch, a smith repairs a stranger's weapon. The player gradually learns whose example they are following.

## 3. Quest pacing and progression

A main quest is a complete dramatic problem with several stages, a change in understanding, a demanding playable encounter, and an aftermath. Talking to Maelis, opening a gate, and delivering a letter are stages, not three main quests.

| Main quest | Proposed title | Level target, entry → exit | First-play target | Main result | Promotion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Road to Wyman** | 1 → 5–6 | 75–105 minutes | Restore a human supply route and discover that someone is hunting a relic. | None |
| 2 | **The Shard from the Marsh** | 5–6 → 10–12 | 90–120 minutes | Recover the Dusk-Piercer Shard and evidence of an organized search for its witnesses. | None |
| 3 | **The Three Who Held the Causeway** | 10–12 → 15–17 | 100–140 minutes | Hear Paul David's account, preserve corroborating evidence, protect its witnesses, and receive the designated promotion. | One promotion after completion |

These are design targets, not measured playtime or guaranteed current XP outcomes. The three quests aim for roughly 4½–6 hours of authored play, excluding optional exploration. Use meaningful encounters and new information to earn that length; remove empty walking, repeated waves, and duplicate explanations during playtesting.

Quests 4–7 must supply the remaining growth toward level 40+, alongside ordinary exploration and optional activities. The critical story route should not require repetitive dungeon or monster farming to reach its intended difficulty. Quests 4–7 need separate XP and content budgets before implementation; they are not four placeholders followed by a level gate.

Calculate each quest's XP budget from the actual XP curve and expected required encounter rewards. Give substantial one-time XP at meaningful stage completions so rescue, investigation, and dialogue remain valid progression. Main-quest XP and items are deterministic; random drops are bonuses. A player who completes optional content can arrive above the target without having progress or earned levels removed.

Level and rank are separate. **Proposed initial rank arrangement:** early guild registration establishes Class E, as in the existing world. The author-specified Quest 3 promotion is then E → D. That destination is a proposal; the fixed requirement is exactly one promotion after Quest 3. Later ranks receive no quest assignments in this document.

## 4. Main Quest 1 — The Road to Wyman

### Dramatic purpose

The player begins with the immediate human reason to fight: Malasugue needs food, Wyman needs medicine, and neither can survive alone. A practical journey uncovers the first deliberate moves of the larger war.

**Starting point:** Sanctuary Terrace and Malasugue. **Quest giver:** Elder Maelis. **Main cast:** Maelis, Captain Hald, Sir Aldric Vane, and a proposed unnamed cart driver who survived the Winter of Chains.

**Initial journal text:** “Wyman's supply cart has not returned. Take medicine to the outpost, find the missing travelers, and learn why the road has gone quiet.”

### Stage 1 — A town that still prepares

The waypoint's awakening draws concern as well as interest. Maelis knows that another hero is useful, but refuses to promise the player a legendary destiny. Around her, people are packing supplies and taking names of relatives outside the walls.

She gives the player medicine, a letter for Aldric, and a specific task: reopen contact with Wyman. Wyman recently reported an unusual object recovered from the marsh, then stopped sending regular reports. She does not know it belongs to Aljay.

Hald checks that the player can survive a fight through a brief practical training encounter: avoid a heavy strike, interrupt an attacker, and recover using the tools available to their class. Alternate controls and ranged builds must work. This is an instruction stage with immediate retries, not an exam that punishes unfamiliar players.

The guild introduction occurs here. Both guilds support the same story route. Maelis's travel advance covers the existing initial registration fee so mandatory enrollment cannot require farming. Registration establishes the initial Class E baseline; it is not a quest-completion promotion.

**Suggested exchange:**

> Player: “Why send someone who has only just arrived?”
>
> Maelis: “Because the guards must stay with the families here. Hald will see that you are ready for the road. I am asking you to reach our people, not defeat an army.”

### Stage 2 — The abandoned cart

Beyond the South Gate, the player finds a cart near Old Mill Crossroads. The medicine compartments have been forced open, but the food was mostly left behind. Three nearby details create a readable investigation:

1. Wheel ruts show the cart was stopped at the crossing rather than overturned in flight.
2. A cut pack contains a Wyman dispatch sleeve; its enclosed report is missing.
3. A survivor sheltering under the bridge says the attackers asked about a black object brought out of the marsh.

The player discovers these within a compact site, in any order. Once all three are recorded, the journal states the deduction explicitly: **the travelers were searched for information, not simply robbed**. The survivor can explain a missed environmental detail; progress never depends on a tiny hidden object.

### Stage 3 — The people at Lantern Fields

Other survivors have reached a farm storehouse. Goblin raiders, taking advantage of the blocked road, are trying to break in. This encounter teaches protection through positioning and threat control, rather than a requirement to kill a fixed number of goblins.

The player first opens a safe route, then either restores the storehouse's damaged ward or defeats the attackers around its entrance. Both methods protect the same essential survivors. Ward parts lie within the encounter area and do not require a particular class or consumable.

A farmer has kept the door open despite the danger. After the fight, she shows the player a hammer mark scratched into its beam. Roydo once repaired that beam during an evacuation and told her how to brace it. She does not deliver his biography. The house and the people still living in it are the evidence that he mattered.

Optional work saves additional food or recovers a family's belongings. It changes later acknowledgments and grants modest extra rewards; it does not decide whether the main quest remains completable.

### Stage 4 — Two enemies at the crossing

At Fen Bridge, a small Descendant delegation demands human control of the crossing be surrendered. Its leader claims the bridge stands over an ancestral Aether channel. Hald's local defenders refuse because surrender would let another power decide which farms receive food.

A Forsaken retrieval band then attacks the delegation, seeking the same channel and the travelers passing over it. Descendants and Forsaken visibly fight each other. The player has a narrower task: clear the cart lane, bring the survivors behind a ward, and break the device drawing power out of that ward.

Neither foreign force becomes a permanent ally because it happens to attack the other. Descendants also block human movement and attack armed defenders who enter their claimed ground. Spatial separation and clear tells keep the three-sided conflict understandable for a new player.

The encounter ends when the retrieval band's **Forsaken Chainkeeper**, a local elite rather than a major commander, is defeated and the device is disabled. The wounded Descendant delegation withdraws with its own objective unmet. The player has preserved a road, not won the Holy War.

**Elite mechanics:** a clearly marked chain pull, a slow heavy strike, and a tether to the device that the player can break. These establish the vocabulary of Forsaken binding without revealing Kethrax's full fight.

### Stage 5 — Wyman counts the living

The cart reaches Wyman. Aldric recognizes the recovered dispatch sleeve and gathers reports from the survivors. Its paper and seal match the outpost's own missing message. Someone knew what the scout had found before the report reached Maelis.

The cart driver places a small provision parcel beside the campfire for “the three who got us across.” Aldric explains only that three heroes saved many of the people now keeping Wyman alive. Their names are absent from the official honor board.

Aldric cannot hand the player the relic immediately. The scout hid it outside the outpost when pursued, and the people sent to retrieve it have not returned. He needs someone to follow the evidence while the experienced defenders keep the camp and bridge secure.

**Closing journal:** “The road is open. The attack was meant to intercept Wyman's report about a relic from the marsh. Aldric's missing scout may be the only person who knows where it was hidden.”

### Completion, rewards, and persistent results

- Required: essential survivors reach safety, the bridge device is disabled, the Chainkeeper encounter is resolved, and the player reports to Aldric.
- Rewards: staged XP, a basic equipment choice suited to the player's build, supplies, Wyman's checkpoint and local services, and the next investigation. No promotion.
- World changes: supply carts and travelers return to the road; recovered survivors appear at Wyman; the farm has visible repairs. Optional food recovery adds supplies to camp displays.
- Checkpoints: leaving town, securing the farm, before the bridge, and reaching Wyman. A failed protection encounter resets that encounter, not the journey.
- Transition: Aldric's missing-scout report directly opens Quest 2. A new catastrophe is unnecessary.

## 5. Main Quest 2 — The Shard from the Marsh

### Dramatic purpose

The player turns a strange object into a specific historical mystery. Someone deliberately buried the truth about the Dawnbreakers, and several present-day powers want control of the surviving evidence.

**Starting point:** Wyman Outpost. **Quest giver:** Aldric. **Main cast:** Aldric, Sabine Kestrel, and the proposed surviving Wyman scout. **Areas:** the existing outpost, a proposed Reedwater fringe route, a ruined waystation, and a small submerged archive.

**Initial journal text:** “Find Wyman's missing scout and recover the relic hidden beyond the outpost. Keep any records that explain who is searching for it.”

### Stage 1 — Conflicting reports

Aldric supplies the scout's last route. Sabine explains that ordinary marsh danger does not account for the missing patrol: their trail was concealed from the road but deliberately marked from below, where an injured scout might crawl.

The player compares a fresh field note with an older official report. The new note mentions a black lance fragment. The old report says no human relics survived the causeway disaster. This is a contradiction worth investigating, not yet proof that the Registry leadership arranged a betrayal.

Aldric authorizes entry to the **marsh fringe only**. The deep causeway remains inaccessible because of flooded crossings and active binding defenses. Opening this small search route must not also enable the existing Kethrax arena transition.

### Stage 2 — Follow the interrupted escape

Three linked sites establish the scout's movements: a cut guide rope, a sheltered dressing station, and the broken ward at the waystation. Each answers a concrete question and points to the next nearby place. There is no arbitrary “collect ten clues” counter.

At the dressing station, the player finds an old human evacuation token reused as a splint. Its markings show that this route existed during the Winter of Chains. At the waystation, Forsaken searchers have left other valuables untouched while tearing apart the record shelves.

The scout is alive but trapped behind a draining ward. Rescuing them is a required playable action, not a reward for having read enough lore. The player interrupts a channeler and destroys two accessible anchors while managing smaller enemies.

Once safe, the scout explains the relic's immediate history: floodwater exposed it beneath the old causeway; they recovered it, were followed, and concealed it in a warded archive before attempting to reach Wyman. The submerged room's ward muffled its Aether signature. The scout could escape through a damaged drain, but could not carry the sealed container through it.

### Stage 3 — The second claimant

A Descendant search party reaches the waystation after the rescue. Its leader identifies the fragment as carrying Tyrant power and demands custody. A Forsaken group strikes before the demand can be settled.

The player can warn the Descendants of a flank or use the fighting to guide the scout through a protected passage. This changes a later exchange and the approach to the archive, not ownership of a mandatory quest item. A player can show compassion without swearing allegiance.

The Descendants' demand supplies a different interpretation of Aljay before his name is known: to them, a human carrying ancient power is evidence of theft. Forsaken orders call that same power property to be reclaimed. The player has already met humans who associate it with rescue.

### Stage 4 — The flooded archive

The archive is a compact dungeon with three distinct tasks:

1. Restore a drainage wheel while avoiding clearly visible surges. The water rises on a readable cycle, not an invisible timer.
2. Recover the evacuation register from a partially collapsed record room. A paper rubbing and a metal register plate preserve essential information even if the room is revisited.
3. Reach the warded container and defeat the **Forsaken Relic Warden**, a proposed retrieval specialist.

The Relic Warden anchors itself to two Aether channels. Leaving both active makes its barrier durable; disabling them opens ordinary damage windows. It periodically attempts to pull the container toward itself, encouraging the player to interrupt or reposition. It cannot permanently steal or destroy the story item.

The dungeon ends with a deliberate inspection of the shard. It is warm. The player briefly sees a black-armored figure bracing a broken lance against chains and hears water striking stone. There is no clear location, complete memory, or usable combat power. The journal labels this an **unexplained impression**, not historical proof.

### Stage 5 — Names in the register

Back at Wyman, the scout identifies the archive as a former evacuation station. Sabine compares the recovered record with the official account. The register records civilians crossing during the hours when the Three supposedly abandoned humanity. Three escort signatures have been scraped away; the testimony of the rescued scout establishes where the page came from, not what each erased name originally said.

The Warden's search order separately demands recovery of the fragment and living witnesses under a recognizable chain seal. It provides evidence of an organized hunt. It does not conveniently explain the enemy's whole plan.

Aldric recognizes part of an escort notation: “P. David — lake route.” He served with Paul David and knows where he lives. This makes Olivar the next destination through evidence and an existing relationship.

**Suggested exchange:**

> Sabine: “The report says the road was abandoned before nightfall. This register records families crossing until morning.”
>
> Aldric: “I was one of them. Find Paul David. If that is what I think it is, he deserves to know it survived.”

### Completion, rewards, and persistent results

- Required: rescue the scout, recover the shard and register, retrieve the search order, and complete the comparison at Wyman.
- Rewards: staged XP, an equipment or crafting reward appropriate to this level band, permanent evidence records, and Aldric's letter of introduction to Paul David. No promotion.
- The shard is a protected quest item: no sale, salvage, destruction, weight penalty, or requirement for a free inventory slot. If an old save has lost its physical item, the new quest state must support restoration.
- The scout remains at Wyman and can repeat their account. The archive remains explorable without refarming main-quest rewards.
- Checkpoints: scout rescue, archive entrance, before the Warden, and return to Wyman. Drainage progress persists after its stage is complete.
- Transition: take the relic and records to Paul David. The new question is personal: **who carried this lance, and why did someone remove the people who saved these families from the record?**

## 6. Main Quest 3 — The Three Who Held the Causeway

### Dramatic purpose

The mystery becomes a relationship with a surviving hero. Paul David gives the player the truth he witnessed, but the player must act on it. The quest ends with protection of people and evidence, followed by the author-designated promotion.

**Starting point:** Wyman, then Olivar. **Main cast:** Paul David, Aldric, Dockmaster Wren Talbot, and the player's guild representative. **Supporting cast:** two proposed local survivors of the causeway evacuation, introduced through the recovered register.

**Initial journal text:** “Bring the shard and the evacuation records to Paul David in Olivar. Learn why the official account does not match the people who survived.”

### Stage 1 — The swordsman by the lake

Paul David initially refuses a conversation about heroes. He has heard invitations to become a symbol before, and knows what public attention can cost other people.

The shard changes that. He identifies the forge work on Dusk-Piercer and tests its response with a technique Aljay taught him. Its warmth follows a pattern he recognizes from Aljay's living Aether. The journal records **Paul David's expert assessment that Aljay is alive**, rather than pretending the player has personally seen his prison.

The player shows him the register and search order. The register matters as much as the weapon: people he helped save are being removed from the history of their own rescue, and the enemy is now looking for surviving witnesses.

> Paul David: “Where did you find this?”
>
> Player: “Under the old crossing. There were names with it.”
>
> Paul David: “Then keep the names. The lance was ours. That night belonged to everyone who crossed.”

### Stage 2 — Paul David’s account

Target: an 8–12 minute sequence of individually paced scenes, with strong visual storytelling and short dialogue. Preserve the existing introduction rule: advance or skip individual scenes, but no single button that bypasses the whole first presentation. Replays can use a scene list. An interrupted first viewing resumes at a saved scene boundary.

**Scene A — Before they were legends.**

Show the Three among ordinary people: Roydo repairing a door with a hammer too large for the job, Paul David showing a young guard how to stand without locking his knees, Aljay returning borrowed tools after a wall repair. These small scenes establish chosen responsibilities before power.

> Paul David: “Roydo remembered where every injured man lived. Aljay remembered who was still waiting for them. I thought remembering the roads would be enough.”

**Scene B — The Night of Black Wings, twelve winters ago.**

Vharzul descends on Malasugue. Roydo braces a failing gate while families pass beneath it. Paul David cuts through the attackers separating the gate from the harbor. Aljay reaches the Tyrant and drives Dusk-Piercer into its heart.

The killing blow transforms him. Black scales spread across his armor and the red aura lashes outward. People recoil. Roydo approaches anyway; Paul David stands where Aljay can recognize him. Aljay turns away from the fleeing crowd and toward the next attack.

> Paul David: “The darkness did not leave him. He learned what it wanted, and refused it. Then he had to refuse it again the next day.”

His restraint remains an ongoing effort, not a single cure. The scene establishes that the title “Forsaken Hero” describes an accusation and appearance, not his allegiance.

**Scene C — Nine winters of human resistance.**

Use a few connected images instead of a victory catalogue: a saved field planted again; Roydo training defenders who cannot lift his weapon; a ship returning because Paul David reopened its route; Aljay remaining outside a shelter until frightened children choose to approach him.

Show a Descendant champion demanding human submission and a separate Forsaken attack taking advantage of the confrontation. The Three protect the same civilians against both claims. Their accomplishments prove that humanity can stand against powerful enemies without becoming either side's subjects.

> Paul David: “We could hold a crossing. The people behind us learned to hold the next one. That was the part I thought would last.”

**Scene D — The order to the causeway.**

Three winters ago, an authenticated Registry order reports a threatened civilian column and an approaching orc force. The Three go because the civilians are real, even though the enemy report has been manipulated.

Paul David remembers Orsolan Vey's authorization on the order. He did not witness Vey negotiate the betrayal. The distinction matters: the player has a serious lead, not complete knowledge of every conspirator.

At the causeway, the Three find refugees, exhausted defenders, and a human ward whose operators bear trusted Accord insignia. A Descendant force threatens the far approach. Forsaken chains are being prepared beneath the water. The sides want different things: Descendants want the crossing and its ancient channel; the Forsaken want Aljay alive; the compromised officials want the Three contained and their own bargain concealed.

**Scene E — The failure.**

Roydo holds the approach while Paul David opens the escape route. Aljay prevents the opposing forces from overrunning the human column. As the last civilians reach the bridge, the Accord operators reverse the ward. Bolts intended to defend the crossing strike inward.

Kethrax enters after the trap takes effect. Binding anchors constrain Aljay's Aether, and the chain-marshal orders his guards to take him alive. His scene establishes competence, cruelty, and a specific command role; it does not introduce him as ruler of the Forsaken.

Paul David attempts to reach Aljay and receives the chain-brand. When he channels power into his weapon, the mark binds his arm and spreads toward his chest. The restriction attaches to his Aether, so changing swords or hands cannot evade it. Ordinary movement and limited noncombat work remain possible.

Aljay drives the broken lance into Kethrax's chest. Kethrax survives behind the prepared bindings but carries the wound forward. The lance tip breaks away into the floodwater; this is the fragment the scout later finds, not a second identical relic.

Roydo smashes part of the crossing to keep pursuit from reaching the refugees. He disappears with the collapsing stone and water. Paul David sees his light vanish; he does not see a body.

> Roydo: “Paul! The west span. They're still crossing!”
>
> Aljay: “Get them home.”

Paul David chooses the people still escaping. He loses his companions and the crossing, and carries the knowledge that another choice would have left those families to die. The story does not frame saving them as cowardice.

**Scene F — What the survivors carried.**

The official story calls Aljay a willing traitor. The Three's names disappear from records. Show the alternate history preserved in people's lives: a hidden hammer token, a repaired gate, parents teaching the route their rescuers used.

Return to Olivar as one of the families from the flashback passes the lake, older now. Paul David recognizes them. They nod to him without ceremony.

> Paul David: “We failed to keep the crossing. We failed to stop what came after. But those children grew up. Some of them hold Wyman now.”

**Scene G — A new lead, not an immediate boss order.**

Paul David identifies the seal on the recovered search order as Kethrax's. He explains that his brand is tied to that commander's binding network. Destroying its source may release the brand, but they must learn how it works before risking another hero.

He recognizes the Black Spire as a rumored Forsaken prison mentioned in old prisoner accounts. He cannot verify Aljay's current location from the shard alone. A cutaway to Aljay's actual prison is withheld in these quests so the investigation still has something to establish.

> Paul David: “Kethrax helped take him. That does not mean Kethrax holds every answer. Find where they moved the prisoners. Find who kept the records. Then we can decide where to strike.”

### Stage 3 — Check the account

The player follows two names or family references from the evacuation register to Olivar residents. One remembers Roydo holding the crossing; the other helped receive the boats Paul David brought out. They independently corroborate the rescue, without knowing the secret ward mechanism or the identities of all the betrayers.

The player also retrieves a harbor arrival record from Wren. Dates and head counts connect the register to actual arrivals. This prevents the whole investigation from resting on a single legendary character's speech.

Each witness has a current life and reason for caution. The player may request public attribution or a protected statement. Both provide the required evidence; attribution changes how the later record is displayed and who receives a public acknowledgment. No dialogue choice accidentally destroys the main story route.

### Stage 4 — The search reaches Olivar

The search order already established that the Forsaken are hunting witnesses. Their existing agents watch the harbor records office. When the player collects the old arrival record, a watcher follows the clerk to the archive where statements are being copied. The attack follows this observable chain of events; the shard does not suddenly become an unexplained tracking beacon.

Wren catches the intrusion and calls for help. The player must get the witnesses into the archive's warded room and stop a **Forsaken Oath-Hunter**, a proposed specialist sent to destroy testimony and recover the fragment.

The encounter has two phases. First, free a tethered witness and disable the suppression lantern blocking the safe room. Second, defeat the Oath-Hunter after the witnesses and essential records are secure. A longer route lets cautious or ranged builds break the lantern from cover; a direct route offers a faster interruption. Both are valid.

Paul David helps by directing evacuation and identifying the binding marks. He can move furniture, carry records, and guide frightened people. When he attempts to draw Stormwake, the brand visibly stops him. He remains useful without secretly solving the combat.

The safe room protects essential witnesses from permanent death after rescue. Failure restarts the encounter from a checkpoint; it does not erase earlier testimony. Optional records affect extra lore and acknowledgments, not mandatory completion.

### Stage 5 — A record that can survive

Paul David dictates the part of the account he can swear to. The witnesses approve their statements. The guild representative makes copies for Wyman and Malasugue, with private identities protected when requested. This is preservation of evidence, not instant worldwide acceptance or an immediate overthrow of the Registry.

The Oath-Hunter's carried transport extract contains one concrete new lead: a prisoner convoy reference whose date matches the aftermath of the causeway. It lacks the final destination. Paul David recognizes the transfer notation and recommends finding the complete custody ledger. That becomes the handoff into Quest 4, whose actual design remains open.

> Player: “Will you come with me?”
>
> Paul David: “As far as I can. I know their marks. I know the roads we used. I cannot give you my sword arm, but I will not leave you to make sense of this alone.”

He becomes an ongoing mentor and source of interpretation, with new conversations after meaningful discoveries. He is no longer just an NPC who issues a boss target and waits for its death flag.

### Stage 6 — Completion and the designated promotion

Quest 3 completes after the archive is secure, the evidence copies are recorded, and Paul David's debrief is finished. The guild representative then conducts the promotion in the same aftermath scene. The player receives it for the whole body of work: restoring the road, rescuing the scout, preserving evidence, and protecting Olivar's people.

The proposed E → D promotion carries the existing emblem and benefits associated with that rank. The guild funds the registration, so no gold shortfall or optional job quota can delay the author-specified event. It grants exactly one rank and never loops into additional promotions because the player also happens to meet later level thresholds.

> Guild representative: “Wyman's report is here. So are the statements from Olivar. You brought people back, kept the road open, and stayed when the archive was attacked. Your service is recorded. From today, you are Class D.”

Paul David stands with the witnesses. He does not declare the player Aljay's replacement. The moment recognizes someone beginning to take responsibility for the people the Three protected.

### Completion, rewards, and persistent results

- Required: finish the first telling, corroborate it with both witness accounts and the harbor record, secure the archive, preserve the statements, and finish the debrief.
- Rewards: staged XP, the single designated rank promotion, the rank's existing equipment access and benefits, a build-appropriate equipment choice, and Paul David's continuing assistance.
- World changes: Olivar's archive contains the corrected local account; survivors recognize the player; guild dialogue reflects the promotion; Paul David discusses current evidence rather than only the past.
- The deep causeway remains closed. The promotion is recognition of service, not proof the player is ready for Kethrax.
- Checkpoints: arrival, each flashback scene boundary, completed testimony, before the archive encounter, and before the debrief/promotion transaction.

## 7. The investigation must remain logically connected

| Evidence | Source | What it establishes | What it does not establish | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The journal separates **Evidence**, **Accounts**, and **Open Questions**. It records where information came from and updates the player's next concrete action. The player does not need to manually guess the writer's intended deduction to progress.

Aljay is central through decisions and traces, not only mentions of his name. His restraint saved Malasugue; his last attack created the relic and wound; his refusal to abandon civilians explains the trap's success; his survival makes the investigation urgent. Roydo's disappearance remains a real uncertainty. Paul David's continuing involvement lets the player question, verify, and build on the past.

## 8. Main-quest feature contract

### Quest records and stage progression

Use stable IDs such as `mq01_road_to_wyman`, `mq02_shard_from_marsh`, and `mq03_three_heroes`. Each quest owns stages with explicit prerequisites, objectives, completion events, rewards, and checkpoints. The HUD displays the main quest title and current task; the journal contains the larger context and completed stages.

Supported objective types needed here: speak, reach, inspect, recover evidence, rescue, disable a device, defeat an encounter, defend an area, and submit a report. Quest completion comes from validated stage state, not merely entering a map or exhausting an NPC's conversation options.

Investigation sites within one stage can resolve in any order. Later quests cannot complete from future flags acquired out of sequence. Players may visit safe towns early and receive appropriate conversations without triggering the Quest 3 flashback or promotion prematurely.

### Rank rules controlled by the author

Maintain a separate explicit promotion schedule. For this scope, the only entry is “Quest 3 completed → one promotion,” with the proposed E → D mapping. Quests 1 and 2 have no promotion reward. Quest 8 has no scheduled promotion merely because it contains Kethrax.

Levels, ordinary rewards, exploration, boss kills, guild transfers, and repeated dialogue must not invent new promotion milestones. Audit the current automatic promotion loop and the existing Kethrax and Hollow Warden deed gates during implementation. Future milestones stay unassigned until the author supplies them; do not silently retain the old story-based sequence as the new design.

Initial guild registration is handled separately from promotion. Normal progression after Quest 3 must be balanced for the available rank until another promotion is designated, including equipment access. Do not require a future rank that this roadmap has not authorized.

### Persistence and reward integrity

Every completed stage, clue, choice, and reward has a saved identifier. Stage rewards are granted once. Completing Quest 3, granting its reward, and recording its promotion must form a recoverable transaction: a reload can finish an interrupted transaction but cannot duplicate XP, items, or rank.

Cinematics do not directly substitute for quest completion. First-view progress and replay progress are separate. Replaying Paul David's story cannot grant rewards, open the deep marsh, or alter promotion state.

Essential evidence uses protected quest storage and a journal copy. Evidence recovery must work with a full bag, after death, and after returning to an area. Optional choices do not remove mandatory evidence.

### Party play

If these quests support the existing multiplayer mode, specify per-character eligibility and credit. An eligible participant in the encounter receives the evidence without racing another player to pick it up. A late arrival cannot automatically skip missing prerequisite stages just because the host has completed them.

The host controls shared encounter state; each eligible character records their own quest rewards and promotion claim. Disconnection at a checkpoint is recoverable. Local or agreed party cinematic behavior must not silently advance another player's first viewing or rank.

### Presentation

Use readable labels: **Main Quest**, **Current Task**, **Evidence**, **People**, **Completed**, and **Rank**. Show recommended level separately from rank. Essential information must remain readable at normal desktop size; avoid tiny uppercase lore captions.

Map markers guide the current actionable task. Investigation areas become exact markers after examination or a helpful NPC explanation. Dialogue always offers a short reminder of the next step. World changes and witness appearances make outcomes visible without requiring every player to read every journal entry.

## 9. Implementation roadmap for this limited scope

### Step 1 — Establish the new narrative source of truth

Record the author's corrections in `docs/LORE.md` and point to this proposal. Reconcile the old united enemy alliance with the proposed three-sided war before writing final in-game dialogue. Preserve the Three's established identities and distinguish author facts from proposed additions.

Do not continue presenting the old level-6 Kethrax objective or Hollow Warden victory as the end of the campaign in new documentation, guide text, or promotion descriptions.

### Step 2 — Build substantial quests and reliable state

Replace the flat errand presentation in `game/src/core/objectives.gd` with main quests containing stages. Extend the existing dialogue, save, and objective systems only as needed for the objective types above. Implement protected evidence and one-time rewards before adding cinematics that depend on them.

Prepare a separate migration for existing saves. Old flags such as `mq_shard_taken` and `mq_three_told` record a much shorter flow; they cannot prove the new rescues and archive defense occurred. Preserve earned inventory, XP, ranks, and recorded victories. Map only genuinely equivalent discoveries, provide a clear continuation point, and never demote an existing character to make the new opening fit. Characters already at or above the proposed Quest 3 reward rank keep their rank and do not receive an invented extra promotion.

### Step 3 — Implement Quest 1 end to end

Reuse Malasugue, Westreach, Lantern Fields, Fen Bridge, and Wyman. Add investigation interactions, survivors, bridge conflict, and the Chainkeeper. Validate the complete route, including class-neutral solutions and the permanent return of travelers, before expanding Quest 2.

### Step 4 — Implement Quest 2 end to end

Add the marsh fringe and compact archive, the scout rescue, readable rival-faction behavior, the Relic Warden, and the evidence comparison. ### Step 5 — Implement Quest 3 end to end

Rewrite `data_npcs_legend.gd` and expand `cs_the_three.gd` around the account above. Retain the existing hero models, weapons, visual effects, and scene-by-scene controls where suitable. New scenes require staging, animation, subtitles, sound, and saved resume points; increasing dialogue length alone will not create the intended story.

### Step 6 — Balance and verify the complete three-quest opening

Measure first-play time and XP on a story-focused route and a route with optional exploration. Test representative melee, ranged, and caster builds, recovery after deaths, early town visits, reward duplication attempts, save/load boundaries, and eligible party play. Adjust rewards and encounter density toward the level targets without adding repetitive chores.

Revisit `data_guilds.gd`, `guild_rules.gd`, and `HeroData` promotion checks so the old level/deed loop cannot award extra ranks. Audit map recommendations, shops, enemy stats, guide text, and equipment requirements against the longer opening.

### Acceptance criteria

1. The journal contains three substantial completed main quests, not a separate main quest for every conversation.
2. Descendants and Forsaken fight each other as well as obstructing humans in at least one authored encounter; the story makes their different aims clear.
3. Every required clue has a source, a limited meaning, and a usable next lead.
4. The player understands the Three's failure and has met people whose lives and resistance survived because of them.
5. Paul David's full account is in Quest 3; it does not complete that quest or award a promotion by itself.
6. Quest 3's finished aftermath grants the designated promotion once. Quests 1 and 2, repeated scenes, incidental level gains, and unrelated kills grant none.
7. Kethrax cannot be reached through the old short opening, and he is never described as the campaign's final boss.
8. Death, full inventory, reload, interrupted scenes, and supported party disconnects cannot permanently lose mandatory evidence or duplicate rewards.
9. A story-focused playthrough reaches approximately the intended Quest 3 level range without mandatory repeat farming; measured results, not this document's estimates, determine final tuning.
10. The ending presents the prisoner-transfer investigation as the next lead. It does not prematurely reveal Aljay's exact location, resolve Roydo's fate, or conclude the Holy War.

## 10. Boundary for the remaining road to Kethrax

Only these requirements are reserved for Quests 4–7: follow the custody trail, establish what can actually be verified about Aljay, investigate the binding defenses, earn a viable route to the causeway, and develop the player toward level 40+. Each needs its own human stakes and resolution when the author commissions it. No titles, final bosses, promotions, or fixed plot outcomes are assigned to those quests here.

Quest 8 will pay off that preparation by confronting Kethrax as one commander in a much larger war. His defeat can break his contribution to Aljay's imprisonment and release Paul David's brand, but it cannot by itself free Aljay, restore all erased history, or decide the struggle among the three sides. Those consequences belong to the later campaign.
