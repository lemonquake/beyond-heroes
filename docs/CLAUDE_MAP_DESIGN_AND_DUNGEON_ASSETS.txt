ART DIRECTION AND COMPOSITION

Retain the game's grounded fantasy appearance: weathered timber, worked stone,
worn fabrics, distinct regional vegetation, substantial architectural silhouettes.
The Kenney source previews are much brighter and simpler than existing gameplay.
Strip unintended KHR_materials_unlit behavior, avoid plastic shine, reduce toy-like
saturation, apply shared game materials deliberately, and compare next to the hero
and current buildings at the actual gameplay camera. Do not turn the whole game
into the source packs' cartoon sample scenes. Do not blanket a palette texture with
one brown material and lose all object parts. Keep one/two shared material surfaces
where practical. Small accents may keep flat colours after palette correction;
large close-view surfaces should match existing timber/stone/cloth treatment.

Design in three scales:
- Large: coast/cliff shape, canopy groups, terrace edges, bridges, landmark silhouettes
  and negative space determine where the player is and where movement leads.
- Medium: a workshop, garden, boat landing, burial bay or collapsed room is a
  deliberately arranged group with a purpose, rather than a random scatter field.
- Small: tools, bottles, produce, books, offerings and ground transitions reward
  nearby observation. Reduce these first on phones; do not remove main landmarks.

Use an approach view -> landmark -> destination rhythm. Every authored area must
have a clear route, a readable focus and a reason for its contents. Asymmetry,
varied spacing and limited believable wear improve quality without new effects.
Avoid identical pot/barrel pairs outside every doorway and uniform trees on a grid.
Clutter belongs near walls, edges and furniture, leaving central paths and combat
space clear. A bench faces a view or an activity. Firewood sits near its user.
Produce is beside a farm or vendor; wet cargo and repairs are beside a landing.
Foliage follows moisture, sunlight and disturbance. Bare, maintained town paving
does not need dense grass; use narrow soil beds and actual garden boundaries.

Keep clear visual hierarchy in night/dark scenes. Lift floor visibility sufficiently
to read threats, steps, pickups and interactables on a small screen. Maintain warm
local light against cool ambience where the current theme calls for it. Use shared
ambient/material response and restrained static shading to ground props on Low;
atmosphere cannot depend exclusively on SSAO, bloom or volumetrics being enabled.
Avoid coloured fog obscuring enemies, glowing clutter mimicking loot, and bright
lights that flatten materials. Preserve all Zarael magical glow/runes as WHITE.
Non-emissive jade/ochre/vegetation can be coloured; ordinary fire keeps fire colours.
Do not colour the Heartwire green, cyan or pink because a source kit uses that hue.

DUNGEONS — REQUIRED THEME AND ATMOSPHERE PASS

Every theme gets a small material/prop/lighting/ambient palette and purpose-driven
room roles. The shared generated builder must support authored role rules without
breaking the floor plans. Keep current plans, height changes, basins, bridge cells,
stair directions, floor IDs, seals, boss/champion placement, loot, growth and returns.
Build atmosphere on the existing multi-storey diorama; do not flatten it into a
single floor or put the downloaded complete room kits over its navigation lattice.

Room roles to author and reuse with deterministic variation:
- Arrival/vestibule: visible theme landmark, safe return point and clear route.
- Work/storage/support: objects show what this part of the dungeon used to do.
- Burial/study/ritual/organic chamber: choose the actual theme-specific function.
- Traversal feature: basin, bridge, gallery, broken vista or stair approach with
  strong level separation and safe parapet/cutaway readability.
- Seal chamber: entrance and guardian movement are clear; clutter stays peripheral.
- Boss approach/arena: one memorable silhouette and quiet negative space that lets
  the existing fight, mechanics and telegraphs remain the focus.

Do not repeat all six functions on every tiny floor. Select roles matching room
shape/lore; even small floors should have a distinctive entry and goal. Reuse motifs
with spacing/condition changes as depth increases, not ever-growing clutter density.
Make room roles data-driven per theme and floor; record their seed and exclusions.
Protect _used cells, arrival, descent/exit, stairs, narrow bridges, chests, seal packs
and boss positions. A blocked side room is not acceptable because the main exit works.

ENGINE IMPORT AND MATERIAL PITFALLS — REQUIRED FIXES

1. MapBuilder.library_mesh currently finds only the first MeshInstance3D, duplicates
   its mesh, and does not apply that child's source transform. Multi-mesh or
   transformed source objects can lose pieces or be the wrong size/orientation.
   Prepare a single identity-transform mesh with correctly applied child transforms
   for batched decor, or implement and validate a multi-mesh-aware batching system.
   Preserve all required surfaces; don't just select the first primitive/material.
2. ENV_DIR currently resolves res://assets/environment/%s.glb. Explicitly prepare
   source-to-runtime names (e.g. kd_tree_oak, kd_barrel, kd_urn_round), avoid existing
   filenames, and record mapping. Don't assume nested vendor models resolve by stem.
3. Imported materials are not named BH_*. MaterialLibrary relies on a material-name
   contract. Map parts to suitable existing shared BH_* materials or a small explicit
   imported palette registry. Preserve theme cache isolation, push_theme/pop_theme
   order and reset state when moving between dungeons and towns.
4. Existing source sizes are small kit units, not guaranteed game metres. Read the
   exact per-model AABB. Target broadleaf trees roughly 4–8m, palms 5–10m, shrubs
   0.4–1.5m, chairs around 0.45m seat height, benches around 0.45–0.5m seat height,
   table surfaces around 0.75m and ordinary doors matching the game's hero. These
   are initial art targets, then compare against real game characters/buildings.
   Apply source pivot offsets: some origins are beneath the object's visible base.
   Use Y-up, intended front +Z, ground contact and appropriate height on platforms.
5. Inspect any source animation/skinning (coffins/gates/flags may have it). Static
   decoration derivatives should not carry unnecessary AnimationPlayer or skeleton
   nodes. Reuse current interaction/state logic for doors/chests/breakables. A
   decorative mesh is not automatically interactable or a new loot source.
6. Existing _flush_batches uses 24m CHUNK cells. Retain spatial subdivision and good
   bounds; don't merge a whole map into one giant MultiMesh. For stacked Agdao tiers,
   consider height-aware subdivisions only where measurements justify them.
7. LITE_KEEP only knows the older kit names. Register retention for EVERY new decor
   name/class so Low doesn't draw 100% of new grass/moss/bottles by accident. Use
   stable position/seed filtering to preserve main silhouettes and coherent groups.
   Landmark, collision and important interaction presence must not vary by quality.
8. Do not duplicate materials for each placement. Keep surface/material counts
   visible in the model report and scene census; meshes with many surfaces increase
   draw calls even when their triangle counts are tiny.
9. Separate collision from visual detail. Use simple static shapes only on meaningful
   barriers, trunks, large rocks and furnishings players actually encounter. No
   per-bottle/candle/moss collision or triangle-mesh collider per tiny prop. All
   obstacle geometry must participate in the established navigation setup; keep
   decorative terrain/overhangs from accidentally carving large navmesh holes.
10. Shared builders are high-impact. Add targeted regression coverage for new multi-
    mesh preparation, material isolation, deterministic dressing/exclusions and
    quality invariants; don't write hundreds of tests that merely mirror coordinates.

PERFORMANCE BUDGETS AND DECISIONS

Set baselines from the actual current source state before changes. Historic PC
performance in output/beta-20261002/perf already shows dense-combat problems; it is
not permission to spend more frame time. Budget by active gameplay view, not total
downloaded asset count. The following are starting limits for NEW environment work;
verify and tighten where device measurements require. Existing scenery should be
optimized alongside additions when needed to stay within the whole-scene budget.

- Frequently repeated tree: aim <=600 triangles; focal tree <=1,200, very sparse.
- Repeated shrub/ground tuft/small debris: aim 20–160 triangles; one surface preferred.
- Repeated rock <=200; focal boulder <=500. Reuse 3–5 silhouettes per region.
- Repeated small prop <=250, large furniture <=600, boats/focal wreck <=2,500 after
  preparation. Avoid multiplying large full ship meshes behind every town.
- Common object one/two material surfaces; shared opaque materials preferred.
- Shared textures generally 256–512px, up to 1,024 only for justified close-view
  architectural/hero props. Keep source atlases shared, use correct mipmaps, audit
  Android compression/VRAM rather than shipping 4K maps on dozens of props.
- Low: roughly 6–10 active repeated decor types per 24m cell, thin tiny clutter and
  undergrowth aggressively (20–35% of High), retain 70–85% of canopy silhouettes,
  use no new decorative local shadows, no SSAO/volumetrics dependency and no dense
  transparency layers. Check actual surfaces when counting types/batches.
- Initial allowance for new visible scenery on Low: <=25 extra draws and <=30,000
  triangles per sampled gameplay view; target replacing expensive existing objects
  or recovering their cost so whole-scene performance stays level or improves.
- New optional per-map animated decor nodes <=8 active nearby, optional emitters <=3
  nearby and <=96 particles total on Low; fewer if profiling shows overdraw.
- New optional positional lights on Low: zero by default; reuse current capped
  lights. On High allow only a few focal lights with distance culling and a measured
  total budget. Emission does not require an extra shadow-casting light per object.
- Set an initial additional prepared environment texture memory budget <=16MiB on
  the mobile profile and additional warm runtime scene memory <=32MiB; measure
  engine/GPU memory and report compression. These aren't promises derived from ZIP
  size. Keep packed release growth <=20MiB for this pass unless measured quality
  gains justify a documented revision, and exclude all unused source formats.

Use identical routes, resolution, renderer, actors, particle/lighting settings and
warm-up. Capture uncapped frame times (cap obscures available headroom), then test
the normal 30fps mobile cap for pacing. Compare median/p95/p99, CPU/script/physics,
GPU, draws, triangles, node/instance count, memory and map load/navmesh time. Run at
