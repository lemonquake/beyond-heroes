# bh-029 environment kit brief (Builders K1 and K2)

You build environment assets for the Godot 4.7 game Beyond Heroes (`A:\Python\beyond-heroes`) for its new island,
**Zarael** — read `work/lemondev/bh-029/contract.md` first (story, theme, frozen asset names, material names, house
rules). Everything is generated by Python run in Blender 5.2: no third-party meshes, deterministic, no manual edits.
The Orchestrator places your assets in the maps; you deliver GLBs that satisfy this brief.

## Theme
Ancient, stepped, monumental stone in the spirit of Mesoamerican architecture — **visual only, no real-culture names**:
stepped pyramids and talus-and-panel terraces, stepped-fret and glyph relief bands, serpent-head balustrade ends,
feathered-serpent statues, carved stelae, flat roofs, lime plaster painted ochre and red, jade and turquoise mosaic
accents, obsidian. Over all of it, the **ancient engineering of the Wirewrights**: gold/copper **wires inlaid in carved
channels** that glow (material `BH_Wire`), engraved **glyph inlays** that glow teal (`BH_Glyph`), bronze-and-stone
pylons with coils, lift frames, aqueducts. It must read as old and hand-built — weathered edges, chipped corners,
moss in joints (`BH_GlyphStoneDark` / `BH_Moss`), no clean sci-fi panels, no neon tubes, no flat colour placards, no
lettering. Every structure must have an evident purpose. The gameplay camera is high (≈50–55° pitch, 16–30 m away):
silhouettes, steps and glowing lines must read from there; keep tiny detail out of the budget.

## Read first
- `tools/blender/environment/README.md`, `kit.py`, `registry.py`, `build_assets.py` (your module is already imported
  by `build_assets.py` — the try/except block at the top), and two or three existing modules close to your work:
  `assets_arch.py` (walls, stairs with ramp collision, arches), `assets_town.py`/`assets_town2.py` (houses), `assets_crypt.py`
  (bridges, temple facade, statues), `assets_dungeon.py`, `assets_guildhouse.py` (a big building), `masonry.py`.
- How the game uses kit assets: `game/src/world/map_builder.gd` (`kit`, `arch`, `decor`, `scene`, `socket_pos`) and
  `game/src/world/material_library.gd` (`ENV` — the `BH_*` names; the Orchestrator adds the new ones from the contract).
- Use the new material names from the contract verbatim (`BH_GlyphStone`, `BH_Wire`, …). In Blender give them a plausible
  preview colour; Godot replaces them by name.

## Conventions (the game relies on them)
- Metres, Blender Z-up exported as glTF Y-up; **Blender −Y becomes Godot +Z, the "front"** (faces the camera). Origin at the
  bottom centre unless the asset spec says otherwise. Architecture fits the 4 m grid.
- Collision: `<name>-colonly` children (boxes/convex pieces) exactly as the existing kit does. **Everything a hero should
  walk on must have walkable collision**: terrace tops, stairs as smooth **ramp** colliders (≤ 40°), bridge decks, pier
  decks, footbridges, the pyramid's front stair and tiers. Railings/parapets get thin blocking colliders.
- Sockets: child empties named as listed per asset (`light`, `flame`, `top`, `npc`, …); the game reads them with
  `MapBuilder.socket_pos`.
- Budget per asset: small props ≤ 3k tris, buildings ≤ 15k, landmarks (pyramid, ship, gatehouse, Dawn Engine,
  colossus) ≤ 35k.
- Deterministic: seed from the asset name (the kit does this).

## Validation and evidence
- Build only your own names: `"C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe" -b --factory-startup --python tools/blender/environment/build_assets.py -- <your names or your category>`.
  (It appends to `build_log.txt`; that is fine.) Never build everything; never rebuild other builders' assets.
- Each new GLB needs a `.import` next to it: copy a sibling environment GLB's `.import`, change only the paths, and delete
  the `uid=` line (Godot assigns one).
- Godot import check on a **scratch copy only** (never on `game/`): make a minimal project in your scratch dir, copy your
  GLBs in, run `"C:\Users\Lemon PC\Desktop\Godot.exe" --headless --path <scratch> --import`, check for errors.
- Look at your work: `render_assets.py` (EEVEE) — iso views at the gameplay angle and a 3/4 close view. Up to 3
  revision passes on your own renders. Prioritise: correct scale/origin/collision, readable silhouette, theme, detail.
- Evidence dir `work/lemondev/bh-029/evidence/kit_town/` (K1) or `kit_wild/` (K2): a contact sheet PNG of all assets at the
  gameplay angle, a few close renders, and `assets.md` (name, size in m, tris, materials, sockets, collision notes).

## Rules
- Write ONLY: your module, your GLBs + `.import` files, your evidence dir, scratch under `work/lemondev/bh-029/scratch/<you>/`.
- Do not edit other builders' modules, shared kit code (`kit.py`, `masonry.py`, … — import from them, copy a function into
  your module if it needs changes), `material_library.gd` or any game code.
- No Godot on `game/`, no killing Godot/Blender by image name.
- Final message: a concise report — per asset: name, footprint/height, origin, sockets, walkable surfaces and their
  heights, tris, and anything the Orchestrator must know to place it.
